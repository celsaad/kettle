Support

Kettle is free. All of it.

Every feature is unlocked for everyone — no paid tier, no subscription, no ads, no trial. If you want to put something in the tip jar anyway, there is one inside the app.

Why there's nothing to buy

Kettle has no backend. There is no server to run, no database to host and no per-user cost to recover, so charging a monthly fee would mean charging rent for software that costs nothing to keep running. A subscription delivers something recurring; Kettle doesn't, so it doesn't have one.

Ads are out for a simpler reason: every ad network on Android is a tracking SDK. Putting one in would end the claim that the app collects nothing and transmits nothing, which is the point of the whole project.

What the tip jar is for

Publishing on Google Play costs a one-time developer registration fee. The tip jar exists to cover that, and to make it possible for people who want to say thanks to do so. That's the entire ambition — it is not a revenue model, and Kettle's roadmap doesn't depend on it.

There are three tip sizes, priced in your own currency by Google Play. They are consumable, which means you can tip more than once if you're so inclined — there is no "already purchased" state to get stuck behind.

Tipping unlocks nothing. There is no supporter-only feature and there will not be one. In particular, export stays free forever — gating your own data behind a payment would contradict the reason the app exists.

Where to find it

Open Kettle, go to Settings, then Support Kettle. Purchases are handled entirely by Google Play; payment details never touch the app, and nothing about the transaction is sent anywhere by Kettle. The app records only a local count, so it can say thank you the next time you open that screen.

If the store can't be reached, the screen says so and carries on. A tip jar that can't load is not allowed to break the app.

Free ways to help, which are worth more

  • Report what's broken. A precise bug report — what you did, what happened, what you expected — is more useful than a tip. The issue tracker is public.
  • Share a library. The format is plain YAML and merges by id, so a good program is something you can hand to someone else as a file. Post one.
  • Translate it. The app ships English and Portuguese. Adding a language means one JSON bundle plus a translated starter library — the repo documents exactly which files.
  • Tell someone. There is no marketing budget and no analytics telling me what works. Word of mouth is the whole distribution strategy — the Play listing is the link to send.
  • Rate it on Play. Free, and it does more for a listing with no ad spend behind it than a tip does. A review saying what you actually use it for is worth more than a star on its own.
